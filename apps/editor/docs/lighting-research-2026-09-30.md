# Realistic lighting without sacrificing editing performance

Research date: 30 September 2026. Model: GPT-6.1 Sol (high). Source audit: shared `main` at `b8f6e3e`, before the concurrent lighting corrections in this task. Installed Three.js: **0.186.1**; `package.json` permits `^0.186.1`. This is a research report, not a claim that the proposed techniques are implemented or benchmarked.

## Recommendation

Keep the existing WebGL raster renderer for navigation and editing. First remove invented illumination and make lighting independent of camera mode. Then improve source visibility and introduce **cached, measured indirect lighting**. Prototype the already-installed `LightProbeGridWebGL` against a surface-lightmap alternative before choosing the production GI path. Consider path tracing only as an optional settled, in-viewport quality mode, with immediate raster fallback during interaction.

The largest visible improvement is likely to come from coherent light origins, believable shadowing and room-local bounce, rather than additional ambient intensity or postprocessing. That is an engineering judgment based on the code audit; it has not been measured in this task. No source establishes a universal “photorealistic at 60 FPS” setting for this editor.

Installed ceiling lighting is a different case from the invented evening fills. `layoutCeilingDesign()` requires authored ceiling metadata and an eligible room; its emissive plaster approximation scales with design brightness and the room switch level. The panel's broad rectangle is explicitly marked an indirect approximation and follows the same source. This approximation is source-aware, but self-lit plaster is not spatially varying reflected illumination. It should eventually be replaced by the GI cache, with its existing source relationship preserved.

The window rectangles are a deliberate quality/performance compromise. Three.js states that `RectAreaLight` has no shadow support and works with PBR materials. Increasing their intensity does not solve occlusion. [Three.js RectAreaLight documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html).

The HDR/PMREM sky provides useful roughness-dependent reflections, but a scene-wide environment does not know whether a point is in a sealed room. Preserve the photographed sky for outdoor reflections/background, avoid double-counting a bright captured sun plus a separate directional sun, and let room-local GI supply interior diffuse bounce. The current photo sky clips the captured sun and desaturates its lighting copy; these are aesthetic choices, not radiometric calibration. PMREM filters environment radiance for material roughness; it does not solve interior visibility. [Three.js PMREMGenerator documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PMREMGenerator.html).

## Existing optimization evidence to preserve

[The repository performance report](render-performance.md) recorded a disposable 20-object scene at 1100×650 CSS pixels, balanced quality, DPR 0.5, with 24 warmup and 120 measured frames per case on 26 September. Its cached-shadow/adaptive-AO comparison reported overview median draw calls **1,657 → 1,084**, Inside **811 → 239**; CPU render submission **4.5 → 4.1 ms** and **2.0 → 1.5 ms** respectively. Observed frame interval remained **8.3 ms** in both cases. These historical CPU/refresh-limited observations do not prove current GPU time or performance in a furnished flat.

Current code retains demand-driven rendering, shadow invalidation on caster/light changes, half-width/half-height AO during motion, full quality after a 140 ms settle, SMAA, bounded sky caches and a 150 ms sky-capture debounce. Keep them. The source also records an M4 Pro/ANGLE/Metal MSAA comparison at 1890×1422: empty-flat intervals of approximately 16.6 ms at 0×, 38 ms at 2× and 57 ms at 4×. That comment is historical evidence without a complete reproducible run attached here; it supports testing SMAA first, not predicting those numbers elsewhere. The actual HDR render target currently has zero MSAA samples despite old documentation describing 2×/4×.

The sun map is currently 4096² in both qualities. Raising a 2048² map to 4096² creates **four times as many texels**, not four times the measured runtime. Tight light-space fitting is more valuable than indiscriminate resolution increases. Point-light shadow capture requires six scene directions; spotlight capture needs one. Prefer a small cached spotlight budget for directional fixtures, and reserve cubemap point shadows for sources whose omnidirectional visibility materially matters. [Three.js shadow manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html).

## Options and tradeoffs

| Option | Realism gain | Interactive cost | Editing limitation | Position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coherent sources + cached direct shadows + existing GTAO/PMREM | Removes unexplained glow, improves contact and window sunlight | Existing pipeline; deleting unused fills reduces work | No true diffuse interreflection; global IBL/area-light leaks remain | Ship first |
| Indirect-only surface lightmaps for the shell | Soft bounce/color bleeding with wall-aware baking; stable across cameras | Texture sampling plus existing direct lighting | Requires nonoverlapping UVs, rebakes after geometric/material/source edits | Strong alternative for settled architecture |
| Cached room-aware irradiance volumes | Diffuse bounce reaches moved furniture without repacking its UVs | Several texture fetches per fragment; expensive captures when dirty | SH is low-frequency; thin-wall interpolation leaks; volume assignment requires care | Prototype in current WebGL stack |
| Screen-space GI | Dynamic diffuse information while visible | Additional depth/color tracing and temporal reconstruction | Missing/offscreen surfaces, disocclusions and camera-history artifacts | Secondary enhancement after stable world-space lighting |
| WebGPU clustered direct lighting + optional voxel/probe GI | Many authored lights without global top-eight omission | Cluster build and renderer-dependent shading/volume work | Migration of custom GLSL materials/postpasses; direct clustering does not solve occlusion | Separate, benchmarked architecture project |
| Progressive path tracing after settling | True multi-bounce visibility, soft source shadows/reflections | BVH setup and many sample passes; convergence/denoising cost | Noise/reset during edits; material adapters; capability fallback | Optional fidelity mode, not initial navigation renderer |

### Surface lightmaps

`MeshStandardMaterial.lightMap` consumes prebaked illuminance using a second UV set. Bake **indirect light only** while keeping authored direct sources dynamic; otherwise raster direct lighting is counted twice. An assumed RGBA16F 1024² atlas is **8 MiB** before mipmaps/temporary targets; 2048² is **32 MiB**. These are format arithmetic, not observed allocations. Multiple day/night or source-group bases multiply storage. [Three.js MeshStandardMaterial documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshStandardMaterial.html).

Shell geometry needs stable nonoverlapping `uv1`, sufficient texel density and seam padding. Existing world-coordinate finishes are not a lightmap unwrap. Walls rebuilt around opening edits invalidate the relevant atlas. A moved table invalidates its old indirect occlusion; retain real-time direct shadows and either refresh nearby indirect texels or avoid baking movable furniture into the initial shell cache.

The installed `ProgressiveLightMap` accumulates texture-space lighting for semi-static objects and mutates material lightmaps/UVs. It is useful for soft accumulated shadows, not a ready-made multi-bounce GI solver. Use a disposable bake projection, preserve material hooks and do not let bake objects become scene truth. Its accumulation needs explicit invalidation and bounded sampling rather than an idle loop. [Three.js ProgressiveLightMap documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/ProgressiveLightMap.html).

### Irradiance volumes already available in r186

The installed `LightProbeGridWebGL` captures cubemaps and projects L2 spherical harmonics entirely on the GPU. It supports ranged baking (`start`, `count`, `pass`), allowing work to be split over frames; complete a direct pass before subsequent bounce passes. This is a current WebGL option, not a hypothetical WebGPU port. Keep probes in open room air, avoid probes inside walls and test small explicit resolutions instead of meter-derived defaults. [Three.js r186 probe-grid source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/examples/jsm/lighting/LightProbeGridWebGL.js).

Calculated example: an 8×3×8 grid has **192 probes**, thus **1,152 scene-face captures per pass**; one additional bounce yields **2,304**. The installed RGBA32F atlas occupies `Nx × Ny × 7 × (Nz+2) × 16` bytes: this example is **26,880 bytes (26.25 KiB)**, excluding bounce snapshots, the batch target, cubemap/depth storage and shared resources. A small persistent atlas therefore does not imply a cheap bake. Capture work depends strongly on draw count. These are source-derived counts, not millisecond measurements.

The receiver shader uses seven 3D texture fetches from one atlas sampler and a normal offset of half a probe spacing. It interpolates without visibility moments; bright neighboring rooms can bleed across thin walls. A grid crossing the entire flat is unsuitable without leakage testing. [Three.js r186 receiver shader](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lightprobes_pars_fragment.glsl.js).

Multiple grids are selected by **mesh origin**, taking the first bounding volume containing that origin; the single-grid case applies it regardless of bounds. Existing floor/ceiling vertices can use global coordinates with origins near zero. Room volumes therefore require deliberate mesh-origin or per-material binding adapters, particularly for walls spanning rooms. Rectangular bounds also do not match concave rooms automatically. [Three.js r186 WebGLRenderer source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js).

For an initial experiment, use one sealed rectangular room, known sources, an opaque bake shell and a 4×3×4 grid. Capture only after lights/geometry settle and freeze a source snapshot for the entire pass. Compare full-light output with and without existing ambient/IBL/ceiling-emissive terms to prevent double-counting. Invalidate on paint changes as well as geometry/light changes. Discard a bake when its scene/light fingerprint is stale; camera motion alone must not invalidate it. Keep lighting caches disposable rather than adding them as a second authoritative scene format.

Visibility-aware DDGI is a stronger long-term solution to interpolation leaks. Its published formulation adds ray-traced scene visibility and moment-based filtering to irradiance probes. The installed SH grid lacks those mechanisms; applying the name “DDGI” to it would overstate its capabilities. Implementing visibility-aware fields is substantial renderer work, so use the paper to guide a prototype and acceptance tests rather than assume a plug-in will fix walls. [Majercik et al., DDGI research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2019-05_dynamic-diffuse-global-illumination-ray-traced-irradiance-fields).

### Many lights and WebGPU

The r186 `ClusteredLighting` addon partitions the view frustum and evaluates lights reaching each cluster. Its default capacity is 1,024 point lights, 32-pixel tiles, 24 depth slices and 64 lights per cluster; these are capacities, **not supported performance guarantees**. It imports `three/webgpu` and is not a drop-in replacement for this WebGL editor. [Three.js r186 clustered-lighting source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/examples/jsm/lighting/ClusteredLighting.js).

`DynamicLighting` batches analytic-light uniforms so source-count changes need not recompile materials. It likewise requires the WebGPU renderer. This could replace the practical pool's omission policy after a migration. Porting the existing finish `onBeforeCompile` shaders, GTAO customization, cutaway/selection passes and profiler is part of that cost. Clustering culls irrelevant lights; it does not make area lights shadowed or stop light through a wall. [Three.js r186 dynamic-lighting source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/examples/jsm/lighting/DynamicLighting.js).

### Path tracing

The current upstream `three-gpu-pathtracer` README inspected on this date uses WebGPU compute shaders and BVH acceleration; it requires WebGPU, supports standard/physical materials, and requires MIS for punctual lights. Earlier indexed release notes describe WebGL versions, so choose an explicit tested release rather than copy a tutorial across APIs. Its current examples include an interior and denoising/upscaling. [Path tracer primary repository](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer).

For this product, use the same editable 3D viewport, rasterize during dragging/walking, and accumulate only when settled. Stop after a bounded convergence target and return to idle. Reset on actual scene, material, source or camera changes. Existing finish hooks need a compatible material translation; a standard-material-only conversion that removes parquet, paint or tile details is not equivalent output. Tiny emissive strips inside a largely indirect interior are a challenging convergence case, so benchmark this flat rather than extrapolate from an exterior demo. No convergence time or FPS has been measured here.

## Source, material and exposure discipline

Current service brightness is divided by 50 before passing to a `PointLight`. That is a visual scale. Three.js point intensity is candela and power is lumens; an isotropic 800-lumen emitter has `800/(4π) ≈ 63.7 cd`, rather than the current 16. This is illustrative arithmetic only: the saved brightness field is not documented as measured lamp output, and a shade/spot distribution changes the relationship. Do not silently reinterpret existing data as photometric truth. Keep inverse-square decay, document preview units and add real fixture data through an explicit contract when available. [Three.js PointLight documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PointLight.html), [Filament physical light formulation](https://google.github.io/filament/main/filament.html).

Use a common exposure and white balance across camera modes. An explicit user camera/exposure choice can be separate view state, but entering Inside should not secretly brighten a room. PBR Neutral is reasonable for comparing finish colors; ACES/AgX are alternative photographic looks requiring calibrated comparison. Tone mapping cannot manufacture correct visibility or reflected light. [Khronos PBR Neutral project](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping).

Preserve linear HDR shading and exactly one final display transform. Mark albedo/emissive color textures as sRGB and data textures appropriately; inspect imported materials before changing exposure to compensate for them. Use realistic roughness and normal strength, restrained shiny surfaces, physical window frames and small bevels where visible. PBR material detail and geometric edge response matter alongside GI. [Three.js color management guide](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/color-management.html).

## Staged implementation and acceptance

1. **Source correctness and mode parity.** Remove orphan evening-room fills. Use identical sun/sky/ambient/exposure/white balance and postprocessing configuration across 3D, Inside and lit Top. Keep physical roof/wall shadow blockers independent of cutaway appearance. Make light and shadow budgets deterministic by semantic source identity, not camera position. Preserve actual switches and enabled fixtures. Stable top-eight selection fixes camera dependence but still omits sources; record that limitation.
2. **Direct visibility.** Measure single-room night illumination with one installed lamp. Prefer cached spots for downlights; use real fixture direction and source geometry. Budget limited point shadows where necessary. Test leakage before increasing brightness, shadow resolution or AO. Window rectangles remain an approximation pending local GI; do not reintroduce retired window spotlights wholesale.
3. **Indirect-light prototype.** Compare a small probe volume with an indirect-only shell atlas on the same flat. Measure bake time, input response during ranged updates, settled GPU cost, sampler use and wall leakage. Pick the technique that passes actual edit/movement tests. Start without decorative emissive ceiling bounce and global interior diffuse fills so improvement is attributable.
4. **Optional renderer expansion.** Evaluate WebGPU clustered lighting when actual source-count omission is visible. Evaluate settled path tracing only after material compatibility and a capability fallback exist. Keep current interaction speed and source/history semantics as acceptance conditions.

Observable checks should include midnight with no fixtures (dark, apart from explicit outdoor/environment illumination); one light on versus off; a sealed neighboring room; opening/closing an opaque door; stable selected light identities during all cameras; sunlight through glass rather than removed roofs; a moved opaque cabinet; changed wall paint; no stale cache after cancel/undo/model loading; context/disposal and idle rendering. Compare world-space surfaces or underlying uniforms where possible: perspective-dependent specular response is legitimate, camera-dependent source replacement is not.

Performance protocol: use an actual furnished flat and a simpler control scene; record browser, OS, GPU, Three revision, quality, drawing-buffer size and DPR. Warm 24 frames, then measure at least 120 contiguous interaction frames along a repeatable path. Report median/p95 CPU update, submission, frame interval, draw calls, triangles, material programs, textures and geometry. Use the existing `?profile` GPU timer queries only when supported and non-disjoint, reporting missing/delayed samples. Frame interval is a user-visible metric affected by refresh scheduling, not a GPU duration. Separately record cold load/shader hitches and GI bake duration. A 60-Hz 16.7 ms or 30-Hz 33.3 ms target is a proposed budget, not a measured result.
